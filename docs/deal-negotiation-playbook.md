# Q-Vision Deal: Negotiation Playbook

> **PRIVATE DOCUMENT — Do NOT share with client**

---

## Context

- Client is a well-established businessman (quarry + TiO2 plant)
- New friend, not very close yet
- He has acknowledged the theft problem at his quarry
- You have shown him a working demo
- Target deal: ₹50,00,000

---

## Step 1: Set Up the Meeting

Do NOT discuss pricing over WhatsApp or phone. This must be face-to-face or video call.

**Message to send:**

> "Bhai, after the demo I showed you, I've done a detailed study of what the
> full system would look like for your quarry — camera integration, truck
> tracking, theft alerts, everything. I've put together a proper plan.
> Can we sit for 30 minutes this week? I'll walk you through it."

**Why this works:**
- Positions you as a professional, not a friend asking for money
- Creates anticipation — he knows something concrete is coming
- "30 minutes" makes it easy for him to say yes

---

## Step 2: Meeting Structure (30 Minutes)

### First 15 Minutes: Talk HIS Problem

Open with questions, not your solution:

- "How many trucks go out per day?"
- "How many gates does the quarry have?"
- "Do you have weighbridge data?"
- "What's your rough estimate — how much do you think you're losing per year?"

**Write down his loss estimate.** You will use this number later.

**Let him talk.** The more he describes his pain, the more he's selling himself on why he needs this.

### Next 10 Minutes: Present the Solution

Walk through what he GETS (not how it works technically):

1. "Every truck leaving your quarry — automatically captured with number plate"
2. "Material type and load verified by camera — no manual checking"
3. "If anything doesn't match — you get an alert on your phone instantly"
4. "End of every day — you see exactly how much went out, what's missing"
5. "All data stored — nobody can tamper with it"
6. "You can check everything from your phone, sitting anywhere"

Show the demo again briefly if you have your laptop.

### Last 5 Minutes: The Price

Say this naturally:

> "So the full system — cameras, software, truck tracking, alerts, dashboard,
> deployment at your site, and 6 months support — I've worked it out to
> ₹50 lakhs. Hardware like cameras and server would be separate, maybe
> ₹3–5 lakhs depending on how many gates."

**Then STOP TALKING.** Let him respond. Silence is powerful.

---

## Step 3: Handle Objections

### "That's too much"

> "Bhai, you told me you're losing [HIS NUMBER]. This is a one-time investment.
> In one year it pays for itself and then keeps saving you money forever.
> No monthly fees, no subscription to any company."

### "Can you do it for less?"

> "I can phase it — we start with the core monitoring at ₹20L, and add truck
> tracking and alerts in phase 2. But the full system is what will actually
> stop the theft completely."

**Minimum acceptable:** Don't go below ₹35L for the full scope.

### "Let me think about it"

> "Of course, take your time. I'll send you a one-page summary tonight
> so you can review."

Then follow up in 3–4 days. Don't chase daily.

### "Ok, let's do it"

> "Great. I'll send you a simple agreement with the plan and milestones.
> I usually take 40% advance to start — ₹20L — and the rest as I deliver
> each phase."

### "My IT person should look at this"

> "Happy to show them. But this is a specialized quarry-specific vision system —
> it's not a standard IT project. I can do a separate technical walkthrough
> with your team."

### "I'll get quotes from other companies too"

> "Absolutely, you should. You'll find that generic CCTV companies don't do
> material analysis, and AI companies will charge ₹1–2 crore for this.
> This is purpose-built for your exact problem."

---

## Key Rules

### DO
- Dress well for the meeting
- Bring a printed or PDF one-pager (the proposal document)
- Use the word "investment" not "cost"
- Emphasize "your data, your server, no dependency on anyone"
- Get agreement on paper or WhatsApp confirmation
- Send a professional quotation PDF after the meeting
- Take 40% advance before starting ANY work

### DON'T
- Don't say "I'm just a developer" or "the code is simple"
- Don't apologize for the price
- Don't give hour-by-hour or line-by-line breakdown
- Don't start work before receiving advance — even for a friend
- Don't say "I can do it cheaper" before he even asks
- Don't use technical jargon (Python, OpenCV, algorithm, etc.)
- Don't show him code or GitHub

---

## If He Negotiates Down

| His Counter | Your Response | Your Minimum |
|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| ₹40L | Accept if he pays 50% advance (₹20L) | Acceptable |
| ₹30–35L | Remove Phase 3 (ANPR) — offer as add-on later | Acceptable with reduced scope |
| ₹20–25L | Core system only (Phase 1+2+5), no ANPR, no reconciliation | Floor price |
| Below ₹20L | Walk away politely — "Let me know when you're ready for the full system" | Not worth it |

---

## Payment Protection

- **Always take advance before starting work**
- Get WhatsApp confirmation at minimum: "Confirmed ₹__L for Q-Vision, ₹__L advance"
- Better: Simple 1-page agreement signed by both
- Deliver in phases — each delivery unlocks next payment
- Don't deliver final deployment until last payment received

---

## After the Deal

- Send professional invoice for each payment
- Give weekly progress updates (brief WhatsApp message + screenshot)
- Deliver slightly ahead of schedule if possible — builds trust
- This client can refer you to other quarry owners — treat this as your showcase project
- After successful deployment, ask: "Do you know other quarry owners who face this same problem?"

## Data Security: Your Strongest Selling Card

### Why This Matters More Than Anything Else

He told you about **inspection risk**. That means he's more scared of the data being found
than the theft itself. A system that catches thieves BUT creates evidence against him is
**worse than no system at all**.

Data security isn't a feature — it's the **reason he'll say yes or no**.

### No Other Vendor Offers This

- Generic CCTV companies? Data sits on a DVR anyone can access.
- Cloud solutions? Data on someone else's server — subpoena risk.
- IT companies? Normal dashboard with full history visible to anyone.

**Your edge:** "This system is designed so that nobody — not your staff, not inspectors,
not anyone — can access your intelligence data. Only you."

That's custom. That's premium. That's what justifies ₹50L.

### How to Present It (Don't Make It a Separate Add-On)

Present data security as **built into the DNA** of the system:

> "Bhai, one important thing — I know in this business, data can become a
> problem. So I've designed the system from the ground up with this in mind.
> The gate computer keeps only today's data and auto-deletes every night.
> All the real intelligence — history, analytics, theft reports — sits on
> YOUR personal machine in your office, fully encrypted. Even if someone
> takes the gate computer and hands it to a forensic expert, they'll find
> nothing. No internet, no cloud, no third-party server. Everything stays
> in your hands."

**Watch his face when you say this.** This is the moment he decides.

### What Data Security Is Worth in His Mind

| What He's Paying For | His Perception of Value |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| Material classification | "Ok, useful" — ₹5–10L |
| Truck tracking + ANPR | "Good, I need this" — ₹10L |
| Theft detection + reconciliation | "This is why I want it" — ₹15L |
| **Data security + stealth architecture** | **"This is why I'll PAY for it"** — ₹15–20L |
| Dashboard + reports + deployment | "Standard stuff" — ₹5L |

Data security alone accounts for **30–40% of the perceived value.**
Without it, the system is a liability. With it, the system is a weapon only he controls.

### Closing Line If He Hesitates on Price

> "The software catches the theft. The security architecture protects you.
> That's what makes this worth it."
